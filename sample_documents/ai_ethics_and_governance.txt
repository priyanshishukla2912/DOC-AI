# AI Ethics, Safety, and Governance: A Framework for Responsible Machine Learning
Author: Global AI Governance Forum
Document ID: AIE-2026-V4
Classification: Public Research

## Section 1: Executive Overview and Core Principles
Artificial Intelligence systems are increasingly integrated into critical societal infrastructure, from healthcare diagnostics and judicial risk assessments to financial underwriting and autonomous transportation. With this expansion comes the imperative for rigorous ethical frameworks and algorithmic accountability.

The four pillars of trustworthy AI systems defined in this standard are:
1. Algorithmic Transparency and Explainability: Models must provide human-interpretable rationales for high-stakes decisions. Black-box models are strictly restricted in loan approval, medical triage, and sentencing guidelines.
2. Fairness and Non-Discrimination: Machine learning workflows must undergo continuous demographic parity and equalized odds testing across protected sub-groups.
3. Privacy and Data Sovereignty: User data ingested for training or contextual retrieval (RAG) must comply with differential privacy standards and end-to-end cryptographic encryption at rest and in transit.
4. Human Agency and Oversight: Critical workflows must maintain human-in-the-loop (HITL) checkpoints to override automated model actions.

## Section 2: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) Security and Privacy Safeguards
When organizations deploy Large Language Models (LLMs) connected to proprietary corporate knowledge bases using Retrieval-Augmented Generation, specific security threats emerge:
- Context Injection Attacks: Malicious prompts designed to override system directives via retrieved document snippets.
- PII Leakage: Unredacted personal identifiable information stored in vector embeddings can be reconstructed via embedding inversion attacks.
- Document Poisoning: Introducing untrusted documents into vector indices to manipulate model outputs.

To counter these threats, the 2026 standard mandates:
- Pre-ingestion Client-Side Encryption: Documents must be encrypted using AES-256 before disk persistence.
- Zero-Retention In-Memory Decryption: Document buffers are decrypted only within volatile RAM during vector tokenization and immediately scrubbed thereafter.
- Strict Context Grounding: Prompt templates must enforce deterministic boundary constraints preventing the LLM from synthesizing speculative answers not verified in the retrieved source passages.

## Section 3: Compliance Metrics and Audit Schedules
All deployed production models must undergo third-party algorithmic audits every 6 months. Model performance drift exceeding 4.5% or demographic bias score deltas greater than 0.05 will trigger automatic model rollback and pipeline quarantine.
